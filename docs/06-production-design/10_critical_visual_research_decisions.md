# Critical Visual Research Decisions

Status: `PRODUCTION_RESEARCH_V1`

This dossier resolves the immediate visual-development questions needed for storyboards and Draft V2. “Resolved” means a defensible production rule has been established; it does not mean that an exact historical object, building or route has been proven.

## Evidence discipline

1. The original Tamil text and HWT concordance control narrative facts.
2. Archaeology supplies bounded evidence for materials, crafts, water systems, exchange and settlement practice.
3. Comparative history may support a plausible range.
4. Screen geography, blocking and prop mechanics are labelled adaptation decisions.
5. A design cannot be described as historically exact unless the evidence supports that claim.

## 1. The paired anklets and courtroom proof

### Source locks

- Kaṇṇaki controls a paired set.
- One anklet travels into the attempted sale and state custody; the other remains with Kaṇṇaki.
- The queen's anklet is associated with pearls; Kaṇṇaki's with gems.
- Material difference, publicly revealed, defeats the accusation.

### Production decision

Build a matched exterior system whose contents cannot be distinguished by casual viewing. Maintain:

- two intact Kaṇṇaki hero anklets;
- a custody duplicate;
- a breakaway courtroom version;
- calibrated gem scatter pieces;
- a pearl-content queen reference;
- safe rehearsal and impact variants.

The courtroom break is deliberate and technically prepared. The gem near the king is material impact, not supernatural motion or light.

### Research boundary

No known excavated anklet is claimed as the epic's exact object. Archaeological evidence for beads, precious stones, gold wire, shell/glass bangles and specialist craft supports a material vocabulary, not a one-to-one reconstruction.

## 2. Madurai court geometry

### Source locks

The scene must keep visible:

- Kaṇṇaki and the surviving anklet;
- the recovered sale anklet;
- king and queen;
- gate/guard and transmission of authority;
- officials or attendants implicated in procedure;
- the sceptre;
- public or institutional witnesses to the test.

### Production decision

Use a legible rectangular or axial hall with three functional depths:

1. threshold and guarded entry;
2. administrative witness zone;
3. royal decision zone.

Kaṇṇaki crosses each boundary through speech and evidence. The anklet test occurs in shared sightlines, not behind a screen or in an off-camera chamber. The fallen sceptre and scattered gems occupy the same final field.

### Research boundary

No exact early-Pāṇḍya palace plan is asserted. Brick, plaster, roof-tile, post-hole and water-system evidence from the Vaigai region informs construction vocabulary only. The final court plan is a declared cinematic interpretation serving procedural clarity.

## 3. Three circuits and embodied accusation

### Source locks

- Kaṇṇaki circles Madurai three times.
- The act is bodily, public and accusatory.
- It precedes the fire command.
- Her injured, exhausted body remains continuous from the Road.

### Production decision

The three circuits are not three identical laps. They cross three civic systems:

1. royal/administrative authority;
2. market and artisan economy;
3. household, sacred and vulnerable civic life.

Each circuit enlarges the number and diversity of witnesses. Distance, breath, foot pain and the weight of the surviving/broken evidence remain visible. The exact route is a designed screen geography tied to the production map, not a claim about excavated ancient streets.

## 4. Selective fire

### Source locks

The fire follows prior moral command and protects the source-named categories. It is not random urban combustion and not permission for uncomplicated revenge spectacle.

### Production decision

Map the city by ignition, spread, refuge, evacuation and protected continuity. Show selection through:

- fire diverting at defensible spatial boundaries;
- citizens guiding protected persons and animals;
- intact islands of care amid institutional collapse;
- distinct sound fields for danger, rescue and withdrawal;
- visible consequences for households and workers.

Avoid magical force fields, anonymous mass destruction, triumphant destruction montage or collapsing every resident into collective guilt.

## 5. Costume and personal material continuity

### Source locks

Costume tracks occupation, status, bodily change and access to resources. Kaṇṇaki's arc must move from merchant-household competence through travel damage and court claim to later communal sacred memory. Mādhavi's costume is professional equipment; Kauṇṭi's objects are practical.

### Production decision

Use fibres, fastening, beadwork, shell/glass ornaments, precious/semi-precious materials, dyes and craft marks as a historically bounded vocabulary. Every hero costume receives state cards for:

- clean/maintained;
- working/performance;
- travel;
- injury/repair;
- ritual or institutional transformation.

No full costume is called archaeologically proven. Avoid homogenised “ancient India,” later royal silhouettes and moral colour-coding.

## 6. Source-specific sacred imagery

### Source locks

Indra-festival Pukār, Aiyai/Korravai hunter ritual, Murukaṉ-linked hill practice, local Pattini recognition and Vañci's installed institution are distinct systems. Hill communities recognise Kaṇṇaki before Cēra monumentalisation.

### Production decision

Give each tradition its own:

- materials and offerings;
- spatial threshold;
- sound and movement grammar;
- participants and labour;
- relationship to political authority.

The Pattini image develops through testimony, stone choice, transport, consecration, craft and recurring worship. Do not prefigure a finished state icon before local recognition.

## External evidence consulted

The following official Tamil Nadu Department of Archaeology pages support bounded production research:

- [Poompuhar](https://www.tnarch.gov.in/poompuhar): waterfront brickwork, timber elements, water movement and maritime finds. It does not prove the epic's exact city plan.
- [Keeladi](https://www.tnarch.gov.in/keeladi): brick structures, ring wells, roof tiles, lime plaster, post-holes, spindle whorls, ornaments, metals and beads. It does not prove an exact royal court.
- [Keeladi ring-well discoveries](https://www.tnarch.gov.in/ring-wells-exposed-ongoing-archaeological-excavation-keeladi): small finds including beads, bangles, gold wire and a beryl weighing stone.
- [Kodumanal](https://www.tnarch.gov.in/kodumanal): bead production, carnelian, metals, weapons and ceramics; useful for craft vocabulary.
- [Karur](https://www.tnarch.gov.in/karur) and the [Chera Site Museum](https://www.tnarch.gov.in/chera-site-museum-karur): trade, inscriptions, coins, precious stones, ceramics, brick and iron in the Cēra-region evidence horizon.
- [Korkai](https://www.tnarch.gov.in/korkai): pearl-fishery and port evidence relevant to the wider Pāṇḍya economy.
- [Alagankulam](https://www.tnarch.gov.in/alagankulam): Vaigai-linked coastal exchange, pottery, beads and brick.
