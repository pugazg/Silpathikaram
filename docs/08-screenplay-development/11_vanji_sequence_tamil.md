## Locked dramatic controls

1. Hill women and their settlement recognise Kaṇṇaki before Cēra royal action.
2. Kaṇṇaki begins Vañci through her own compressed account and retains damaged-body continuity.
3. Kuravar ritual, mountain ecology, Murukaṉ geography and possession practice remain source-specific.
4. Kuravar eyewitness testimony precedes Sāttanār’s historical synthesis.
5. Senguttuvan receives an already completed moral event; he does not create Kaṇṇaki’s truth.
6. Iḷaṅkō Vēṇmāḷ converts received witness into a memorial responsibility.
7. Stone debate, northern vow and state mobilisation retain the tension between memory and royal prestige.
8. Councils, provisioning, scribes, allies and workers make the expedition institutional rather than individual.
9. War remains organised mass violence; wounds, bodies, animals and coercion are visible.
10. The Himalayan stone’s passage across defeated crowns preserves political domination.
11. Ganges consecration transforms the stone but does not purify the expedition’s human cost.
12. Māṭalan reconnects victory to Mādhavi, Kōvalaṉ, Kaṇṇaki, Mādhari, Kauṇṭi and bereaved families.
13. Landscape return songs retain their distinct social and ecological voices.
14. Veteran bodies remain visible beneath triumphal reception.
15. Prisoner release and civic remission precede the completed shrine institution.
16. Ritual experts, teachers, calendrical specialists and craftspeople collectively construct the shrine.
17. Dēvanti and women memory-bearers precede transregional royal adoption.
18. The epic closes with ethical conduct—not conquest—as its final action.
