## V2 corrections completed

1. **Court architecture** — fantasy throne-room ornament was replaced with brick, lime plaster, timber posts, plain hangings and low platforms.
2. **Royal appearance** — oversized crowns, jewellery and dominant ceremonial armour were reduced to restrained status markers.
3. **Anklet scale** — the proof objects now read as matched hollow metal anklets rather than oversized bracelets or magical artefacts.
4. **Material proof** — the break, gem scatter and impact remain physical; no glow or supernatural trajectory is used.
5. **Interval composition** — broken anklet, gems, sceptre and Kaṇṇaki remain in one causal field.
6. **Public vow** — the king was removed from the street confrontation; Kaṇṇaki addresses citizens while referring back to the failed palace system.
7. **Three circuits** — later gopurams and monumental medieval streets were replaced with distinct early-historic administrative, artisan and household environments.
8. **Fire presence** — the giant anthropomorphic fire figure was reduced to ambiguous flame, smoke and heat shimmer.
9. **Selective fire** — low brick/timber/plaster buildings, evacuation, caregivers, cattle and protected continuity remain visible.
10. **Performance continuity** — Kaṇṇaki's injury, fatigue and lack of triumph are sustained across all five sheets.

## Locked visual logic

- Kaṇṇaki crosses three court depths through testimony and evidence.
- The two anklet states remain legible before the test.
- Queen and king witness the same material result as officials.
- Recognition arrives too late and is never framed as restitution.
- Remembered women are invoked through speech and civic memory, not apparitions.
- Each circuit has a different civic function and sound field.
- Protected categories are named before ignition.
- Rescue and survival are part of the fire sequence, not an afterthought.
